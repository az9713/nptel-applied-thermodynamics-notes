Entropy balances in thermal equipment

Close the mass and energy accounts before interpreting the entropy residual in condensers, mixers, nozzles, and coupled devices.

Separate the condenser heat path from pump work

Consider the Tutorial 2 train. Steam condenses at constant pressure, then the liquid flows through an insulated pump. The coolant path ultimately rejects heat to an environment at temperature \(T_0\). Let \(\dot m\) be the water flow rate, with states 1 at the condenser inlet, 2 at its exit and 3 after the pump. The specific enthalpy \(h\) measures internal energy plus the pressure-volume flow term. The specific entropy is \(s\). With negligible kinetic and potential energy changes and no shaft work in the condenser, the signed rate of heat entering at the condenser is \(\dot Q=\dot m(h_2-h_1)\). For the pump alone, the required shaft input is \(\dot W_{\mathrm{in}}=\dot m(h_3-h_2)\). The ideal pump in the exercise is isentropic, so \(s_3=s_2\). Its compression still requires work.

The condenser's wall temperature can vary. For the extended boundary reaching ambient, total generation is \(\dot m(s_3-s_1)-\dot Q/T_0\). If condensation supplies nearly all the heat rejection at saturation temperature \(T_{\mathrm{sat}}>T_0\), the thermal path contributes approximately \(-\dot Q(1/T_0-1/T_{\mathrm{sat}})\). For the tutorial's subcooled outlet at 40 °C and 10 kPa, use the liquid entropy at that state. IAPWS-IF97 gives \(h_1=2583.89\) and \(h_2=167.54\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}\), so 1 kg/s rejects about 2416.34 kW. The stream entropy change is \(0.572429-8.148893=-7.57646\,\mathrm{kJ/(kg\,K)}\). With \(T_0=303.15\,\mathrm K\), generation inside the extended boundary is about \(0.3943\,\mathrm{kW/K}\).

The Gibbs relation \(dh=T\,ds+v\,dp\) reduces to \(dh=v\,dp\) in the ideal pump, where \(v\) is specific volume. Approximating liquid \(v\) by its inlet value gives \(h_3-h_2\approx0.00100788(10000-10)=10.07\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}\). Solving the isentropic pump state with IF97 gives 10.05 kJ/kg, which quantifies the error in holding \(v\) constant over this pressure rise. Tutorial 2, Problem 7 · IAPWS-IF97

Frozen-composition gas mixtures

Problem 8 asks how entropy changes in an ideal-gas mixture with unchanged composition. On a molar basis, let \(y_j\) be species \(j\)'s mole fraction and let \(\bar c_{p,j}\) be its molar heat capacity. With ideal gases and constant heat capacities, \(\bar c_{p,\mathrm{mix}}=\sum_j y_j\bar c_{p,j}\). For fixed composition, the species partial pressures are \(y_jp\), so their pressure ratios equal the total pressure ratio. Weighting the species Gibbs relations by \(y_j\) yields

\[ \bar s_2-\bar s_1 =\bar c_{p,\mathrm{mix}}\ln(T_2/T_1)-\bar R\ln(p_2/p_1), \]

where \(\bar R=8.314\,\mathrm{J/(mol\,K)}\) is the universal gas constant on the same molar basis as \(\bar c_{p,\mathrm{mix}}\). Divide the molar heat capacity by the mixture molar mass to change to a mass basis. If composition varies, the partial-pressure ratios must retain the mole fractions, and the fixed-composition expression no longer applies. Tutorial 2, Problem 8

The nitrogen–neon problem slide gives \(0.5\,\mathrm{mol/s}\) of each gas. The two molecular masses, 28 and 20 g/mol, therefore give a combined flow of 24 g/s. The quoted heat-capacity ratios \(1.4\) and \(5/3\) give molar heat capacities \(3.5\bar R\) and \(2.5\bar R\), so \(\bar c_{p,\mathrm{mix}}=3\bar R\). At \(T_1=300\,\mathrm K\) and \(p_2/p_1=1/8\), setting the entropy change to zero gives \(T_{2s}=300(1/8)^{1/3}=150\,\mathrm K\). The stated turbine efficiency of 0.50 gives \(T_2=300-0.50(300-150)=225\,\mathrm K\). The actual molar entropy rise is \(3\bar R\ln(225/300)-\bar R\ln(1/8)=10.11\,\mathrm{J/(mol\,K)}\) for \(\bar R=8.314\,\mathrm{J/(mol\,K)}\). At a total flow of 1 mol/s in the insulated turbine, this produces 10.11 W/K. Problem 8, inputs and rate conversion

A tight outlet couples energy and continuity

Two air streams enter an insulated steady mixer. Let \(T_j,p_j,C_j,\dot m_j\) denote each port's absolute temperature, pressure, speed and mass flow rate. Port 3 is the sole exit, with cross-sectional area \(A_3\). Fixed-composition ideal air has specific gas constant \(R\) and constant heat capacity \(c_p\). Continuity gives \(C_3=\dot m_3RT_3/(p_3 A_3)\) when pressure is in pascals and \(R\) is in \(\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}\). Use those same SI units in the energy balance, with squared speeds halved in \(\mathrm{J/kg}\). Inserting \(C_3\) produces

\[ \frac12\left(\frac{\dot m_3R}{p_3A_3}\right)^2T_3^2+c_pT_3 =\frac{\dot m_1(c_pT_1+C_1^2/2)+\dot m_2(c_pT_2+C_2^2/2)}{\dot m_3}. \]

For positive inlet temperature the right side is positive. The left side increases strictly from zero for \(T_3\ge0\), giving a unique positive root. With the transcript inputs of \(6\) and \(1\,\mathrm{kg/s}\), \(473.15\) and \(373.15\,\mathrm K\), \(100\) and \(50\,\mathrm{m/s}\), exit diameter \(0.10\,\mathrm m\), and exit pressure \(400\,\mathrm{kPa}\), the laboratory solves for \(T_3\) and \(C_3\). It then evaluates entropy generation from the inlet pressures \(600\) and \(500\,\mathrm{kPa}\) and the calculated outlet state. A mixing-only estimate neglecting exit kinetic energy can fail through a small outlet. Tutorial 2, Problem 9

Nozzle efficiency and a coupled compressor–intercooler

For the steam nozzle in Problem 10, use the measured exit pressure \(p_2\) and inlet entropy \(s_1\) to obtain the reference isentropic exit enthalpy \(h_{2s}\). With negligible inlet speed, nozzle efficiency is \((h_1-h_2)/(h_1-h_{2s})\). From 8 bar and 200 °C to 2 bar, IF97 gives \(h_1=2839.77\) and \(h_{2s}=2584.60\) kJ/kg. The specified 0.95 efficiency gives \(h_2=2597.36\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}\). The adiabatic energy balance gives \(C_2=\sqrt{2000(h_1-h_2)}=696.30\,\mathrm{m/s}\). Both outlet states are in the saturation dome. The actual vapor mass fraction is 0.95054 and generation per unit mass is \(s_2-s_1=0.03243\,\mathrm{kJ/(kg\,K)}\). A mass flow rate would be required to turn this specific result into an entropy rate. Tutorial 2, Problem 10

In Problem 11, air enters at 96 kPa and 27 °C with a volumetric rate of 26.91 m³/min. The ideal-gas relation \(\dot m=p\dot V/(RT)\) gives \(0.49982\,\mathrm{kg/s}\). The insulated compressor delivers air at 263 kPa and 127 °C. At \(c_p=1.005\,\mathrm{kJ/(kg\,K)}\), shaft input is \(0.49982(1.005)(127-27)=50.23\,\mathrm{kW}\). The intercooler brings the air to 77 °C at the same pressure. With water warming from 25 to 40 °C at \(4.18\,\mathrm{kJ/(kg\,K)}\), its required mass flow is \(0.49982(1.005)(127-77)/[4.18(40-25)]\approx0.401\,\mathrm{kg/s}\).

Across the combined control volume, the compressor exhaust and intercooler inlet cancel. The two external changes give total generation of about 15.0 W/K. Treated separately, the compressor gives a residual of about \(-0.12\,\mathrm{W/K}\), which the adiabatic model forbids for exact inputs. The stated outlet pressure is about 0.22 kPa above the isentropic upper limit for the stated outlet temperature, a difference smaller than the reported pressure increment of 1 kPa. An admissible reconstruction needs pressure or temperature precision sufficient to resolve the sign. The positive combined result does not establish admissibility of the compressor alone. Tutorial 2, Problem 11

An adiabatic vortex tube can split a stream by temperature

Problem 12 supplies 1 kg/s of ideal air at 1 MPa, 20 °C, and 1 m/s. Two outlets discharge at 101.3 kPa, each carrying half the inlet flow. The hot outlet is at 116 °C and 100 m/s. The cold outlet has speed 200 m/s. Let \(C_j\) and \(T_j\) denote the port speeds and absolute temperatures. With zero shaft work, negligible elevation changes and the stated insulated boundary, the steady energy balance yields

\[ T_3=2T_1-T_2+\frac{C_1^2-(C_2^2+C_3^2)/2}{c_p}, \]

where \(c_p=1.4R/(1.4-1)=1004.5\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}\) and \(R=287\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}\). Substitution gives \(T_3=172.26\) K. The entropy balance gives \(\dot S_{\mathrm{gen}}=\sum_{j=2,3}\dot m_j[c_p\ln(T_j/T_1)-R\ln(p_j/p_1)]\approx532.39\,\mathrm{W/K}\). The inlet pressure drop allows an adiabatic separation of the temperatures with positive entropy production. These balances check the given outlet split and hot state. Predicting the split itself requires a flow model. Tutorial 2, Problem 12

Exercises and worked solutions

Condenser feasibility

A condenser rejects \(100\,\mathrm{kW}\) at a uniform \(330\,\mathrm K\) to ambient at \(300\,\mathrm K\). Find the entropy generated by that thermal path.

Show solution

Solution. An idealized condenser rejects \(100\,\mathrm{kW}\) isothermally at \(330\,\mathrm K\) to ambient at \(300\,\mathrm K\). The thermal path generates \(100(1/300-1/330)=0.03030\,\mathrm{kW/K}\). An ideal insulated downstream pump adds no entropy generation. Moving the coolant boundary to ambient includes the positive generation even if the condenser fluid balance is internally reversible.

Nozzle reference

A nozzle has \(h_1=3000\) and \(h_{2s}=2800\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}\), efficiency \(0.90\) and negligible inlet speed. Find actual exit enthalpy and velocity.

Show solution

Solution. Take \(h_1=3000\) and \(h_{2s}=2800\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}\) with efficiency \(0.90\). Then \(h_2=3000-0.90(200)=2820\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}\). With negligible inlet speed the exit speed is \(\sqrt{2000(180)}=600\,\mathrm{m/s}\). This is an energy calculation. Phase and entropy require an additional property lookup.

Internal flows

The compressor raises the air entropy flux by 0.04 kW/K. Its intercooler lowers the air entropy flux by 0.03 kW/K and raises the water entropy flux by 0.06 kW/K. How much entropy does the insulated train generate?

Show solution

Solution. If a compressor raises the air's entropy flux by \(0.04\,\mathrm{kW/K}\), its intercooler lowers the air entropy flux by \(0.03\,\mathrm{kW/K}\) and increases the water flux by \(0.06\,\mathrm{kW/K}\), generation for the insulated train is \(0.04-0.03+0.06=0.07\,\mathrm{kW/K}\). The entropy change of the cooling air is negative even though generation of the exchanger is positive.

Restriction and limiting flow

What happens to the mixer outlet temperature if the outlet area grows while inlet properties, mass flows, and exit pressure remain fixed?

Show solution

Solution. The velocity factor \(\dot m_3R/(p_3A_3)\) tends to zero. The quadratic reduces to its linear enthalpy term. Using the chapter inputs, the mass-weighted inlet enthalpy-equivalent temperature is \(458.86\,\mathrm K\), and \((6\cdot 100^2+1\cdot 50^2)/(2\cdot 7\cdot 1010)=4.42\,\mathrm K\) of inlet kinetic energy raises the limiting outlet to \(463.28\,\mathrm K\). This limit assumes the exit pressure can still be held fixed.

The standalone lab checks continuity, energy and the sign of entropy generation for the restricted mixer outlet. It gives \(426.47\,\mathrm K\), \(272.72\,\mathrm{m/s}\), and \(267.61\,\mathrm{W/K}\) for outlet temperature, speed, and generation rate. A second check reproduces the vortex tube's cold-outlet temperature and entropy generation.
