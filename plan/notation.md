# Notation

| Symbol | Meaning | Typical unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $p$ | Absolute pressure unless named otherwise | kPa |
| $T$ | Absolute thermodynamic temperature | K |
| $m$, $\dot m$ | Mass and mass flow rate | kg, kg/s |
| $V$, $v$ | Volume and volume per unit mass | m³, m³/kg |
| $C$, $z$ | Flow speed and elevation | m/s, m |
| $\rho$ | Mass density | kg/m³ |
| $U$, $u$ | Internal energy and specific internal energy | kJ, kJ/kg |
| $H$, $h$ | Enthalpy and specific enthalpy | kJ, kJ/kg |
| $S$, $s$ | Entropy and specific entropy | kJ/K, kJ/(kg K) |
| $Q$, $\dot Q$ | Heat into the system and heat transfer rate | kJ, kW |
| $W$, $\dot W$ | Work delivered by the system and work rate | kJ, kW |
| $x$ | Vapor mass fraction for saturated liquid–vapor equilibrium | dimensionless |
| $T_0$, $p_0$ | Temperature and pressure of the reference environment | K, kPa |
| $T_b$ | Absolute temperature where heat crosses the chosen boundary | K |
| $B$, $\psi$ | Stored physical exergy and specific flow exergy | kJ, kJ/kg |
| $R$, $\bar R$ | Mass-specific and universal molar gas constants | J/(kg K), J/(mol K) |
| $\varphi$, $\omega$ | Relative humidity and water-vapor mass per dry-air mass | dimensionless, kg/kg dry air |
| $\phi$, $\lambda$ | Fuel–air equivalence ratio and actual-to-stoichiometric air ratio | dimensionless |

Subscripts $f$ and $g$ refer to saturated liquid and saturated vapor. The subscript $s$ on a device outlet state denotes the isentropic reference exit at the actual exit pressure. In the nozzle chapters subscript $t$ denotes a stagnation property, superscript $*$ denotes a sonic reference, and $p_b$ is the back pressure. In the psychrometry chapters superscript $*$ instead denotes a quantity per kg of dry air. Cycle state numbers are local to the chapter.

Take signed heat into the chosen system and signed work out. A named input, rejection, or output magnitude is positive. The power cycles use $q_H$ for heat input and $q_L$ for rejection. The refrigerator uses $q_C$ for heat absorbed on the cold side and $q_H$ for heat rejected on the hot side. Lowercase $q$ and $w$ refer to quantities per kg unless a dry-air or fuel basis is stated. An overbar marks a molar property.

Use kelvin in thermodynamic temperature ratios and entropy integrals. Psychrometric correlations identify $t$ in degrees Celsius. With pressure in pascals, $pv$ is in J/kg. With pressure in kilopascals it is in kJ/kg, and divide $C^2/2$ or $gz$ in m²/s² by 1000 before adding it to a specific enthalpy in kJ/kg. The compressible-flow derivations use J/kg and pascals unless stated otherwise.
