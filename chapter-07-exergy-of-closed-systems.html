The reversible comparison uses useful work

Suppose a compressor takes the material inside a sealed piston from state 1 to state 2. Let \(E,S,V\) be its energy, entropy, and volume, and let \(T_0,p_0\) be the environmental temperature and pressure. For a fixed composition and fixed mass, the physical exergy change is \(\Delta B=\Delta E+p_0\Delta V-T_0\Delta S\). The supplied useful work is the signed negative of \(W-p_0\Delta V\), where \(W\) is total work by the material. If all heat exchange is with the ambient reservoir, \(\Delta B=W_{u,\mathrm{in}}-T_0S_{\mathrm{gen}}\). For a compression intended to raise exergy, the product is \(\Delta B\) and the actual useful work input is the resource.

For ideal air with gas constant \(R\) and constant heat capacities \(c_v,c_p\), the initial mass is \(p_1V_1/(RT_1)\). Then \(V_2=mRT_2/p_2\), \(\Delta U=mc_v(T_2-T_1)\) and \(\Delta S=m[c_p\ln(T_2/T_1)-R\ln(p_2/p_1)]\). Use absolute temperature and pressure. The lab takes the tutorial's piston data and approximate constant-property air, defines the reported \(1.2\,\mathrm{kJ}\) as useful work input, and obtains destruction from the balance. Tutorial 4, air compression

State properties and process heat play different roles

For a constant-pressure cooling process with only quasistatic pressure work, use \(Q=\Delta H\) and \(W=p\Delta V\). The tutorial cools 5 kg of R-134a at 0.7 MPa from 60 to 25 °C. The saturation temperature at that pressure is 26.69 °C, so the final state is subcooled. Approximate its \(u,v,s\) with saturated-liquid values at 25 °C. The slide gives initial values 274.0 kJ/kg, 0.0349 m³/kg, and 1.026 kJ/(kg K), and the liquid approximation gives 85.85 kJ/kg, 0.0008286 m³/kg, and 0.3243 kJ/(kg K). Inserting them into \(Q=m[(u_2-u_1)+p(v_2-v_1)]\) gives about −1060 kJ. For the 100 kPa and 25 °C reference vapor, the slide tabulates \(u_0=252.65\) kJ/kg, \(v_0=0.238\) m³/kg, and \(s_0=1.106\) kJ/(kg K). Substituting in \(B=m[(u-u_0)+p_0(v-v_0)-T_0(s-s_0)]\) gives about 124.5 kJ initially and 212.7 kJ finally. When all heat reaches 25 °C ambient, \(T_0m(s_2-s_1)-Q\approx13.9\) kJ is destroyed. Compression by the 0.7 MPa load supplies about 102.2 kJ of useful work, accounting for both the rise in stored exergy and the destruction. Tutorial 4, refrigerant cooling

In the partitioned-tank problem, 1.5 kg of liquid water initially at 300 kPa and 60 °C expands into an equal evacuated compartment. The final pressure is specified as 15 kPa. The outer volume stays fixed, so there is no boundary work. The water's specific volume doubles to about 0.002034 m³/kg. At 15 kPa, \(v_f\approx0.001014\) and \(v_g\approx10.02\) m³/kg, giving \(x_2=(v_2-v_f)/(v_g-v_f)\approx1.02\times10^{-4}\). The final state is nearly saturated liquid. With IAPWS-IF97 properties and the quality retained in \(u_2=u_f+x_2(u_g-u_f)\) and \(s_2=s_f+x_2(s_g-s_f)\), \(Q=m(u_2-u_1)\approx-37.41\) kJ. For a boundary extending to ambient at 298.15 K, \(S_{\mathrm{gen}}=m(s_2-s_1)-Q/T_0\approx0.01223\) kJ/K and \(B_{\mathrm{dest}}\approx3.65\) kJ. The specified final pressure and volume determine the heat transfer. An adiabatic model would instead fix \(u_2=u_1\) and use that constraint to solve for the final pressure. Tutorial 4, partitioned water tank

For a piston moving under a specified pressure with stops, divide the process into a fixed-volume segment and a segment at its load pressure. In the fixed-volume segment \(Q_{12}=\Delta U_{12}\) if there is no other work. In the second \(Q_{23}=\Delta U_{23}+p_{\mathrm{load}}(V_3-V_2)\). The heater's exergy resource is \((1-T_0/T_H)(Q_{12}+Q_{23})\) when a single source reservoir at \(T_H\) supplies all heat and the enlarged boundary reaches it. A reservoir at a temperature different from ambient can supply exergy, even when the piston does little useful work. Tutorial 4, piston stops

The initial cylinder pressure is 0.2 MPa and the lift pressure is 1.0 MPa. One kilogram of water occupies 0.10 m³ until lift. At each of these pressures \(v=0.10\) m³/kg lies between the saturated endpoints, so use \(x=(v-v_f)/(v_g-v_f)\) to find \(u\) and \(s\). The final state at 1.0 MPa and 400 °C is superheated, with \(V_3\approx0.3066\) m³. This gives about 206.6 kJ of total work and \((p_{\mathrm{load}}-p_0)(V_3-V_2)\approx185.9\) kJ of useful work at \(p_0=100\) kPa. The combined heat input is about 2433.5 kJ. Including the source at 673.15 K gives \(S_{\mathrm{gen}}=S_3-S_1-(Q_{12}+Q_{23})/T_H\approx1.696\) kJ/K and \(B_{\mathrm{dest}}\approx505.6\) kJ at 298.15 K. At those endpoints and heat input, the reversible useful work is the actual useful work plus \(B_{\mathrm{dest}}\). The actual-to-reversible useful work ratio is about 26.9%.

Internal heating and fixed-composition mixtures

An insulated tank contains a hot iron block and cooler water while a paddle wheel adds work. Let \(C_b,C_w\) be their constant total heat capacities, \(T_b,T_w\) their initial absolute temperatures, \(T_f\) their common final temperature, and \(W_{\mathrm{in}}>0\) the paddle work. With negligible container heat capacity and volume change, first-law and entropy accounts give

\[ C_b(T_f-T_b)+C_w(T_f-T_w)=W_{\mathrm{in}},\qquad S_{\mathrm{gen}}=C_b\ln(T_f/T_b)+C_w\ln(T_f/T_w). \]

Even if \(W_{\mathrm{in}}=0\), equilibration through a finite temperature difference generates entropy. When the shaft delivers work as well, its energy adds to final internal energy, and dissipation is included in the generation residual. Tutorial 5, iron block, water and paddle wheel

For the constant-composition neon, oxygen and nitrogen mixture in a piston, use mole fractions \(y_j\) and species molar masses \(M_j\) to obtain mixture molar mass \(M=\sum_jy_jM_j\). The mass fraction is \(w_j=y_jM_j/M\), so the mass-specific heat capacity is \(\sum_jw_jc_{p,j}\). The mass-specific gas constant is \(R_u/M\). The volume displaced by travel \(\ell\) in a cylinder of diameter \(D\) is \(\pi D^2\ell/4\). Use the appropriate external pressure history to compute work from that volume change. The piston diameter and travel alone do not fix the pressure history. Tutorial 5, ideal gas mixture

Transfer the same reasoning to flow devices

Source case Exergy balance to apply
Tutorial 5, air compressor Shaft work supplies the stream exergy increase when heat ultimately reaches ambient. The destruction is the difference between the two rates.
R-134a throttle For an insulated steady valve with negligible speed and elevation changes, \(h_e=h_i\) and the physical exergy decrease is \(T_0(s_e-s_i)\). The matched-endpoint reversible device can exchange heat with the environment at \(T_0\) and deliver \(w_{s,\rm rev}=\psi_i-\psi_e\).
Insulated steam turbine Include inlet and exit speeds in stream exergy. The matched-state reversible power exceeds actual power by \(T_0\dot S_{\mathrm{gen}}\).
Steam turbine losing heat Recover wet-steam enthalpy and entropy from quality if the outlet is saturated. Use the energy balance to infer mass flow if actual output and heat loss are given. Use the ambient heat term for the extended entropy generation.
Steam nozzle Kinetic energy increase is the useful product when acceleration is the purpose. Compare it with the thermomechanical stream exergy reduction that supplies it.

In the steam nozzle example, the rounded inlet and actual outlet enthalpies are 3398 and 3214 kJ/kg. With negligible elevation change, the insulated nozzle converts the 184 kJ/kg drop to a kinetic energy increase. Starting at 70 m/s, the outlet speed is \(\sqrt{70^2+2000(184)}\approx611\) m/s. The isentropic exit at the same pressure has \(h_{2s}\approx3186\) kJ/kg, so the ratio of actual to isentropic kinetic-energy gains is \(184/(3398-3186)\approx86.8\%\). Alternatively, at \(T_0=298\) K and \(s_2-s_1=0.045\) kJ/(kg K), the physical exergy drawn from the thermodynamic state is \(184+298(0.045)=197.41\) kJ/kg. The kinetic gain recovers about 93.2% of that resource. The two efficiencies use distinct reference quantities.

Exercises and worked solutions

Ambient work in compression

A load supplies 3.0 kJ of useful work as volume drops by 0.008 m³ in 100 kPa ambient. Find the gas's total work input.

Show solution

Solution. A piston reduces volume by \(0.008\,\mathrm{m^3}\) against \(100\,\mathrm{kPa}\) ambient while a load supplies \(3.0\,\mathrm{kJ}\) of useful work. The atmosphere supplies another \(0.8\,\mathrm{kJ}\), so total work input into the gas is \(3.8\,\mathrm{kJ}\). Use the total in the energy balance and the useful input in the exergy balance.

Thermal equilibration

Two rigid bodies at 400 and 300 K equilibrate in an insulated enclosure. Each has heat capacity 1 kJ/K. Find the lost useful work at 300 K ambient.

Show solution

Solution. Two rigid bodies each have heat capacity \(1\,\mathrm{kJ/K}\) and initially sit at \(400\) and \(300\,\mathrm K\). An insulated enclosure gives \(T_f=350\,\mathrm K\) and \(S_{\mathrm{gen}}=\ln(350/400)+\ln(350/300)=0.02062\,\mathrm{kJ/K}\). At \(T_0=300\,\mathrm K\), the lost useful work is about \(6.186\,\mathrm{kJ}\).

Valve benchmark

How much physical exergy does an insulated valve destroy per kilogram for an entropy rise of 0.08 kJ/(kg K) at 300 K ambient?

Show solution

Solution. If an insulated valve forces a stream through a specific entropy rise of \(0.08\,\mathrm{kJ/(kg\,K)}\) at \(T_0=300\,\mathrm K\), it destroys \(24\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}\) of physical exergy. For the valve alone and negligible kinetic and potential terms, all of the stream exergy decrease is destroyed.

Heater potential and work benchmark

A piston receives 60 kJ from a 600 K reservoir and gains 18 kJ of physical exergy. If ambient is 300 K, what useful output would a reversible process deliver at those endpoints and heat input?

Show solution

Solution. The heat brings \(60(1-300/600)=30\,\mathrm{kJ}\) of exergy. The reversible limit sets generation to zero, so the useful work would be \(30-18=12\,\mathrm{kJ}\). A measured useful work of \(8\,\mathrm{kJ}\) would correspond to \(4\,\mathrm{kJ}\) destroyed.
